How to make a custom chart

Explore answers questions like “in which centuries are the Hoysaḷa records?” or “which dynasties left the most records in Tamil Nadu?”

1. Choose what to count by

Count records by sets the bars: Century, Dynasty, District, State, Language, Material, Type or Corpus. Century bars run in time order; the others run from most to fewest records.

2. Add filters

+ add filter picks a category, then a value. Each value shows how many records it would leave, given the filters you already have. Add as many as you like — a record must match all of them. Remove one with its ×; reset clears them all.

3. Read the chart

  • Hover a bar for its number and share; click it to open exactly those records in Advanced Search.
  • Long lists show the top 25 and group the rest as Others; Table lists every value.
  • Grey bars (Undated, Not recorded) are records the source gives no value for; they are counted, not hidden.
  • The corpus buttons at the top of the page apply here too.

4. Share it

The page address keeps your chart, e.g. AtAGlance.html?by=century&dynasty=Hoysaḷa; copy it to send someone the same chart.

Worth knowing

These count what survives and was published, not what was carved, and they inherit each source’s gaps: see “How these are counted” below for how centuries and dynasties are worked out. Centuries before the 7th often come from misread or regnal dates; check a record before citing it.

How these are counted

  • Source: the records published on this site's corpus pages, as they stand at build time. A record printed in two corpora (an ARE summary later published in full in SII or Epigraphia Carnatica) is counted in each. TN Inscriptions are the Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology's numbered and district volumes together with the Inscriptions of the Pudukkottai State.
  • Century: the first A.D. year in the Date field, or else in the first 300 characters of Remarks; Śaka years +78, Vikrama −57, Kollam +825, "Nth century" as its middle. EpCa also reads "Sake" and "Samvat" years and looks through the whole translation. Regnal years are not converted, so they count as Undated.
  • State: as shown on the corpus pages; Epigraphia Carnatica and Karnatak Inscriptions are Karnataka, TN Inscriptions Tamil Nadu. District: as printed (mostly colonial-era names, lightly merged: Tanjore → Thanjavur and similar); Epigraphia Carnatica by volume.
  • Dynasty: the printed dynasty grouped by keyword (all Chōḷa forms together, Vijayanagara's four houses together). SII often prints the king instead; well-known Chōḷa and Pāṇḍya kings are mapped to their dynasty.
  • Copper plate: ARE's Copper Plate section, or "copper plate" in the place or the start of Remarks. Hero-stone / Sati-stone: the hero-stone word-list classification shown in each corpus page's Type column (Sanjay pages for SII and ARE).
  • Undated, Unknown and Not recorded mean the value has not been read from the record yet, or the book does not state it. They do not mean the inscription has none. Much of Epigraphia Carnatica's dynasty field is still blank, so compare series with care.
  • These are counts of what survives and was published, not of what was carved: a district looks dense where a survey was thorough.
