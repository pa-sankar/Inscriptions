Ādi Śaṅkara in the inscriptions

Research note: records that name him, his works, his image or the institutions named for him. Lifetime c. 788-820 (traditional). Sanjay area, not public.

What the records show (Claude's overview)

The Cholamadevi record is in these books. TaKa Vol. I, No. 9/1990 (two entries: the structured record and its prose overview) is the grant the ASI project cites: under Vīrarājēndra (1063-70) the assembly of Cholamadevi (Tiruchirappalli) gave land to a teacher who expounded the Pradīpaka of Chidānanda, a commentary on the Śārīraka Bhāṣya "known as Bhagavat-pādīyam". It is the only record found here that names his work before the 14th century.

Advaita is named earlier than Śaṅkara himself. In Karnataka a Jaina teacher is praised in 1036 as "an axe to the tree of the Advaita speakers", and a teacher of the Baḷḷigāve maṭha as "the Advaita luminary" (1071); in Telangana a Mallikārjuna is "a great teacher of the Advaita philosophy" (1199). The school is visible as a rival and a teaching before any record names its founder.

Śṛṅgēri from 1346. Harihara I and his brothers grant nine villages for forty brahmins of the maṭha at Śṛṅgēri (1346); Vidyāraṇya receives villages (1375); his successors the Narasiṁha-Bhāratis are endowed through the 15th century, with a library for the maṭha (1376/1407). Vijayanagara grants repeat that the capital was "made by Vidyāraṇya" (1344-1565), and two Śṛṅgēri plates (1450, 1463) give the line "Surēśvarāchārya, disciple of Śaṅkarāchārya, disciple of Bhagavatpāda". Kūḍli plates that call Vidyāraṇya "a part of Śaṅkara" and cite the Śaṅkara-vijaya carry dates the editor himself doubted.

Kāñchī and the Tamil maṭhas, 16th-19th centuries. An Ādiśaṅkara shrine in the Kāñchī temple carries records of 1529-1566; the Kāmakōṭi-pīṭha is built in the 16th century; Śaṅkarāchārya maṭhas appear at Jambukēśvaram (1708), Kumbakōṇam (1710), Aruṇi (1718) and Puṣpagiri; a Kāmakōṭi pontiff from Kumbakōṇam renovates the Kāmākṣī temple (1839).

96 records in all. Not found here: the Gujarat copper plate the article mentions, and anything tying him to a place in his own time.

A stored term category (shankara, terminology/extra_categories.yaml) matches his names and titles in every corpus and script: Rāmānuja, Emberumāṉār, Uḍaiyavar, Yatirāja, Iḷaiyāḻvār, Bhāṣyakāra, Śrībhāṣya, in Roman, Tamil, Kannada and Telugu letters. Claude read every match in context and judged it; the "not him" records are taken out of the category, so the category filter on every page and in Advanced Search shows only the records judged to be about him. Judgments: research/acharya_readings.csv. Dates are the year Claude read in the record, or the site's own date reading; regnal years are not converted.
