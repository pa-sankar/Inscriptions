2026-11-11 · v1.4

New: Epigraphia Carnatica

  • Epigraphia Carnatica, Karnataka's inscription series, is now searchable: all 16 volumes, 11,681 records, with the English translation, English transliteration and Kannada text where the volume prints them. It is in Advanced Search too.
  • Where a volume (XV, XVI, XVIII) prints no translation, one made by AI is shown and labelled as such.

New: At a Glance

  • At a Glance charts every published corpus by century, state, district, dynasty, language and material. Every bar opens those records in Advanced Search.
  • Explore: count by any category, with any filters.

Advanced Search

  • Dropdown filters for Dynasty, District, State, Century, Language and Material; text boxes for King and Place.
  • Pick several values, or leave some out. The ☰ button beside every list opens it as a checklist. Tick several values to find records with any of them, or switch to Exclude to find records with none of them. For example, State → tick Tamil Nadu → Exclude finds every inscription outside Tamil Nadu; Dynasty → type "chal" → tick the four Chāḷukya lines finds all the Chalukyas together. A "Filtering on" line under the filters always shows what the results are using.
  • The filter values you pick are highlighted in the fields they matched.

Search, on every corpus page and in Advanced Search

  • Wildcards. In the Keyword box, * stands for any run of letters up to a space and ? for exactly one: Kul?ttu* finds Kulottunga and Kulōttuṅga.
  • Search in Tamil, Kannada and other scripts. Type the word in its own script (பைரவ, ಭೈರವ). This finds text stored in that script only, so search Bhairava too. Most useful in Epigraphia Carnatica's Kannada text; misread letters mean that no result does not prove absence.
  • Find a record by its code. Each record's 🏷️ button holds its code; paste it into Keyword to go straight to it. With a wildcard a code lists a group: are|1956-57|a|* is all of Appendix A of the 1956-57 report.
  • Embedded, Partial and Full word matching on every corpus page.
  • See where the term matched. The search term is highlighted in the results, in Read More and in every field and tab of the record popup. The popup opens on the tab holding the match, marks every tab that has one, and when a record matches more than once, Prev and Next step from one match to the next.
  • Typing in a filter dropdown narrows its list again, and a number finds Roman-numbered volumes (2 finds II, XII, XX).
  • Language filters list standard names: ARE's 1,512 printed variants are now 31 languages, and a record in several languages appears under each. ARE gains a Script filter. Dropdowns are alphabetical.
  • "Searching…" shows until results are in, and Stop cancels a long search. Every corpus page opens with the same columns; corpus names toggle by clicking them.

Reading a record

  • "Ibid." now says what it refers to, in italics beside it: "Ibid. [Ep. Ind., Vol. XX], pp. 171 ff." The brackets mark it as ours, not the report's. About 1,040 of the Annual Report's 3,583 Ibid references show this so far; the rest are in progress.
  • The record popup has a header with the record's code and a copy button.
  • King shows the name without "who …" and "son of …" clauses. "Roman Transliteration" is now "English Transliteration".

Better data

  • Annual Report on Epigraphy is now named as printed. Pages with shifted columns were read again, and records lost or misnumbered in the first reading are back. Dots in a column now mean blank; only "Do." means "same as above".
  • South Indian Inscriptions: each record now opens on the scan page where its heading is printed (about 4,100 records moved). Place and heading lines moved out of Remarks, dates into Date, with footnote and text clean-ups.
  • Date, King, Language and Dynasty are no longer only what the volume prints. Where a field is blank, it may be filled from the inscription's own text (its Remarks, translation or page scan), and such values are marked as derived, with the evidence in their tooltip.

How it works

  • A new section on Epigraphia Carnatica: how one inscription's three printed parts are joined, and where AI is used.

Coming soon

  • Tamil Nadu inscriptions, including Tamilnadu Manila Kalvettukal and many district-wise (Mavatta) Kalvettukal volumes.
  • Further SII and ARE corrections, including more Ibid references.

2026-10-19

New: Advanced Search

  • Advanced Search searches South Indian Inscriptions and the Annual Report on Epigraphy together, in one box. Narrow it to particular SII volumes or ARE report years, and choose how a word must match: anywhere, at the start of a word, or the whole word. It also finds a record by its ID.

Where each inscription is

  • Every SII record now shows a Location, except the few that have no find-spot by nature (copper plates, for example). Many came from each volume's own contents table and section headings.
  • "Same taluk", "same district" and "same village" are now resolved to the actual names, in both SII and ARE.
  • About 41,000 ARE records now show their modern state, up from under 6,000.
  • "The same temple" or "the same place" in a record's Place/Source or Remarks now shows which temple or place is meant.

More read from each record's own text

  • Where a record's Dynasty, King or Date column is empty but its Remarks name them, the value is now filled in from the Remarks, shown as derived so it's never mistaken for what was printed. How this works.
  • SII Volume XXXII Part II is now published, and records that had been missed or duplicated in several volumes have been recovered or removed.

Search

  • A new search now always replaces the previous results, and no matching record is skipped.

How it works

  • The How it works page is now one section at a time, and has a new section on reading values from the Remarks.

Coming soon

  • Epigraphia Carnatica, Karnataka's inscription series.
  • The Tamil Nadu series: Tamil Nadu Inscriptions (TaKa), its district-wise volumes (DiKa), and Inscriptions of the Pudukkottai State (IPS).

2026-10-09

New: Annual Report on [South] Indian Epigraphy (ARE)

  • A second full corpus is now searchable alongside South Indian Inscriptions: the Annual Report on Epigraphy — the Archaeological Survey of India's own yearly listing of every inscription copied and examined that year, across copper plates, stone inscriptions, and (for the earlier years) pre-1945-46 collections.
  • All 66 published report-years are in (1906 through 1996), 56,000+ records, same searchable table, filters, and record-detail popup as South Indian Inscriptions.

Coming soon

  • Advanced Search — one search box across every corpus on the site at once, instead of searching each one separately.

2026-10-06

Faster, and easier to keep up with

  • The page now remembers what it already loaded and only fetches what's actually changed on a repeat visit, instead of re-downloading everything every time — noticeably faster after your first visit.
  • Subscribe (button above) to hear about future updates to the site as we make them, instead of having to check back yourself.

Smarter search

  • Search now finds a match regardless of accent marks in the transcription — a plain, easy-to-type search term now also finds records whose text carries the full diacritics.

Reading a record

  • Clicking a row now opens a tabbed popup (Details / Remarks) instead of one long block of text — easier to scan.
  • Footnotes are now collected in one place at the end of a record's own text, instead of interrupting the narration wherever they happened to fall on the printed page.
  • Some records now carry a separate Commentary tab — the editor's own historical discussion of a whole cluster of inscriptions (e.g. the Kailāsanātha Temple group in Volume I), previously buried in the scan and invisible anywhere on the site.

New: Sources

  • A new Sources page shows exactly which scan or edition each volume came from, linked from the main table.

Coming soon

  • Annual Report on Epigraphy (ARE) volumes.
